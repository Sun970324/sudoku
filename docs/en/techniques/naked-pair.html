Naked Pair

A Naked Pair is two cells in the same row, column or box whose only candidates are the same two digits. Those two digits can then be removed from every other cell in that row, column or box.

Where to look
One row, column or box
What to find
Two cells with the same two candidates
What to remove
Those two digits in the other cells

The Naked Pair pattern

The diagram shows row 2 only. R2C2 and R2C6 each have just two candidates, 3 and 7. Two cells holding the same two digits and nothing else form a naked pair.

Naked pair (3 and 7) Candidates to remove Candidates that stay

Why does this work?

R2C2 and R2C6 can only be 3 or 7. The two cells share the two digits between them, so there are only two cases.

Case 1If R2C2 is 3, R2C6 is 7.
Case 2If R2C2 is 7, R2C6 is 3.

Either way, the 3 and the 7 of row 2 go to these two cells. So 3 and 7 can be removed from the other cells of row 2 (columns 1, 4 and 8).

How to find the Naked Pair

  1. Look for a cell with only two candidates.
  2. Check the same row, column or box for another cell with exactly the same two.
  3. If there is one, remove those two digits from every other cell of that row, column or box.

If the two cells share both a line and a box, you can remove the digits from both.

Common questions

What if the cells hold 3, 7 and 3, 8?

That is not a naked pair. Both cells need exactly the same two digits.

What if three cells share three digits?

The same idea gives a naked triple. Each of the three cells has to hold only digits from those three.

How is it different from a hidden pair?

A naked pair is two cells with only two digits. A hidden pair is two digits that fit in only two cells. They remove from opposite places.
