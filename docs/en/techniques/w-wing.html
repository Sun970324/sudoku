SUDOKU TECHNIQUE · Wings

W-Wing

A W-Wing is two cells with the same two candidates, joined by a strong link on one of those digits. The other digit can be removed from any cell that sees both cells.

Cells needed
Two cells with the same two candidates
What joins them
A line with two candidates for one digit
What to remove
Cells that see both cells

The W-Wing pattern

R2C2 and R7C8 both hold 5 and 8. Row 5 has only two candidates for 8, R5C2 and R5C8. R5C2 shares a column with R2C2 and R5C8 shares a column with R7C8, which joins the two cells.

The 8 in the 5 and 8 cells The two 8s in row 5 (strong link) The 5 in the 5 and 8 cells 5 to remove

Why does this work?

Suppose R2C2 is not 5.

Then R2C2 is 8R5C2 in the same column is not 8, so row 5 has its 8 in R5C8.
Then R7C8It shares a column with that 8, so it is not 8 and must be 5.

So if R2C2 is not 5, R7C8 is. One of the two cells is always 5, so a cell that sees both (R2C8 and R7C2) cannot be 5. Remove the 5.

How to find the W-Wing

  1. Find two cells with the same two candidates (X and Y) that do not see each other.
  2. Check for a line with exactly two candidates for Y, each one seeing one of the cells.
  3. If so, remove X from cells that see both.

Common questions

What if the two cells see each other?

Then it is a naked pair, which is simpler. W-Wings are for cells that are apart.

Can the strong link be a box?

Yes. A box with exactly two candidates for Y works too.

How is it different from an XY-Wing?

An XY-Wing uses three two-candidate cells. A W-Wing joins two identical pairs through a strong link on one digit.
