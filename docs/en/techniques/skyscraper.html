Skyscraper

A Skyscraper is two columns where a digit has exactly two candidates and one end of each sits in the same row. The digit can then be removed from any cell that sees both of the other ends.

Digits to watch
One
Lines needed
Two columns with two candidates each
What to remove
Cells that see both tops

The Skyscraper pattern

The diagram shows digit 3 only. Columns 2 and 6 each have exactly two candidates for 3. The bottom ends share row 8, while the tops (R2C2 and R3C6) sit in different rows. Two buildings of different heights give the technique its name.

Bottom ends in the same row Tops (the roofs) Cells where 3 is removed 3s that are not affected

Why does this work?

R8C2 and R8C6 are in the same row, so they cannot both be 3. At least one of them is not 3.

If R8C2 is not 3Column 2 has its 3 in R2C2.
If R8C6 is not 3Column 6 has its 3 in R3C6.

Either way, one of the two roofs is a 3. So a cell that sees both roofs cannot be 3. R2C5 shares a row with R2C2 and a box with R3C6, and R3C1 shares a row with R3C6 and a box with R2C2. Remove the 3 from both.

How to find the Skyscraper

  1. Pick one digit and find columns with exactly two candidates for it.
  2. Look for two such columns whose one end is in the same row.
  3. Remove the digit from cells that see both other ends (the roofs).

Rows and columns can swap. If the roofs were in the same row it would be an X-Wing, so they must be on different lines.

Common questions

How is it different from an X-Wing?

An X-Wing's four ends line up as a rectangle. A skyscraper lines up at one end only and is offset at the other.

What does "see" mean here?

Sharing a row, a column or a box. Only a cell that sees both roofs loses the digit.

Are there similar techniques?

The 2-String Kite and the Turbot Fish are in the same family. Each joins two strong links on one digit.
