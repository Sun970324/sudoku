Simple Coloring

Single Chains

Simple Coloring links the lines and boxes where a digit has exactly two candidates and colors the cells in two alternating colors. Any cell that sees both colors cannot hold that digit.

Digits to watch
One
What you need
A cluster of two-candidate lines and boxes
What to remove
Cells that see both colors

The Simple Coloring pattern

The diagram shows digit 7 only. Row 2, column 8 and row 6 each have exactly two candidates for 7, and only one of each pair is 7, so the two get different colors. The colors alternate: R2C2 (green), R2C8 (blue), R6C8 (green), R6C3 (blue).

Green cells Blue cells Cells where 7 is removed 7s that are not affected

Why does this work?

The linked cells take the 7 in turn, so either every green cell is 7 or every blue cell is. You do not know which yet.

R3C3 sees green R2C2 (the same box) and blue R6C3 (the same column). Whichever color is right, one of those cells is 7, so R3C3 cannot be 7. Remove it.

How to find the Simple Coloring

  1. Pick one digit and find lines or boxes with exactly two candidates for it.
  2. Chain those links together and color the cells in two alternating colors.
  3. Remove the digit from cells that see both colors.

If two cells of the same color see each other, that color is wrong. Remove the digit from all its cells and fill in every cell of the other color.

Common questions

How many colors?

Two. Inside one cluster the two colors alternate.

What about two clusters?

Using the relation between the colors of two clusters is Multi-Coloring.

Is it the same as an X-Chain?

It is the same idea. Coloring just looks at every link of the chain at once.
