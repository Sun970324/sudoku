X-Wing

An X-Wing is when a digit's candidates in two rows sit in exactly the same two columns. That digit can then be removed from every other cell in those two columns.

Digits to watch
One
Lines needed
Two rows and two columns
What to remove
That digit in the rest of both columns

The X-Wing pattern

The diagram shows digit 5 only. In row 3, 5 can only go in columns 3 and 5, and row 5 is the same. Those four cells form the corners of a rectangle, and that rectangle is the X-Wing.

The four X-Wing cells Cells where 5 is removed 5s that are not affected

Why does this work?

Row 3 needs a 5, and it can only go in column 3 or column 5. Row 5 is the same. That leaves only two cases.

Case 1If row 3 has its 5 in column 3, row 5 has it in column 5.
Case 2If row 3 has its 5 in column 5, row 5 has it in column 3.

Either way, columns 3 and 5 each get their 5 from these two rows. A column holds only one 5, so the rest of columns 3 and 5 (R1C3, R8C3 and R7C5) cannot be 5. Remove those candidates.

How to find the X-Wing

  1. Pick one digit. Digits that are already placed many times have fewer candidates and are easier.
  2. Find a row where that digit has exactly two candidates.
  3. Look for another row whose candidates are in the same two columns. That is an X-Wing.
  4. Remove the digit from the rest of those two columns.

Rows and columns can swap. If two columns have their candidates in the same two rows, remove the digit from the rest of those rows.

Common questions

Do the four cells have to be in the same boxes?

No. Boxes do not matter. Only the two rows and two columns do.

What if a row has three candidates?

That row cannot make an X-Wing. Three rows sharing the same three columns make a swordfish.

I found the shape but nothing to remove.

If the rest of the two columns has no candidates for that digit, the X-Wing removes nothing. Try another digit or another technique.
