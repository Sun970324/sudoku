Sashimi X-Wing

A Sashimi X-Wing is an X-Wing with one corner missing and candidates (the fin) next to it in the same box. The digit can be removed from cells in the missing corner's column that share a box with the fin.

Digits to watch
One
What you need
An X-Wing missing a corner, plus a fin
What to remove
In the fin's box, in the empty corner's column

The Sashimi X-Wing pattern

The diagram shows digit 4 only. Row 2 has 4 only in columns 3 and 7. Row 6 has a 4 in column 3, but the column 7 corner (R6C7) is empty, and instead R6C8 and R6C9 in the same box 6 hold a 4. Those two cells are the fin.

The three X-Wing cells The fin (R6C8 and R6C9) Cells where 4 is removed 4s that stay

Why does this work?

Split it by whether either fin cell is 4.

If neither fin cell is 4Row 6 has its 4 in R6C3. Then row 2 cannot use column 3, so its 4 is in R2C7, and the rest of column 7 is not 4.
If a fin cell is 4The other cells of box 6 are not 4.

The cells that are not 4 in both cases are in column 7 and inside box 6. So the 4 in R4C7 is removed. R5C7 fits the same rule, but it has no 4 in this diagram.

How to find the Sashimi X-Wing

  1. Find a row with two candidates and a similar row missing one corner.
  2. Check for the row's other candidates (the fin) in the same box as the empty corner.
  3. Remove the digit from cells of that box in the empty corner's column, leaving both rows alone.

Common questions

How is it different from a Finned X-Wing?

A Finned X-Wing has all four corners plus a fin. A Sashimi X-Wing has one corner missing. The logic is the same.

Why "sashimi"?

These patterns are called fish, and this one has a corner sliced off, like sashimi.

What if the fin is in another box?

Then nothing can be removed. The fin must share a box with the empty corner.
