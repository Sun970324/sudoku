Multi-Coloring

Multi-Coloring colors two separate clusters of one digit. When a color of one cluster sees a color of the other, any cell that sees both opposite colors cannot hold that digit.

Digits to watch
One
What you need
Two clusters in two colors each
What to remove
Cells that see both opposite colors

The Multi-Coloring pattern

The diagram shows digit 3 only. Cluster 1 is column 1, where the two candidates are colored R2C1 (green) and R7C1 (yellow). Cluster 2 is column 5, colored R7C5 (blue) and R3C5 (purple). Yellow R7C1 and blue R7C5 share row 7.

Cluster 1 green Cluster 1 yellow Cluster 2 blue Cluster 2 purple Cells where 3 is removed

Why does this work?

Yellow (R7C1) and blue (R7C5) share a row, so they cannot both be 3. At least one of those colors is wrong.

If yellow is wrongIts opposite in cluster 1, green R2C1, is 3.
If blue is wrongIts opposite in cluster 2, purple R3C5, is 3.

Either way, green or purple is a 3. A cell that sees both cannot be 3. R3C3 shares a box with R2C1 and a row with R3C5, and R2C6 shares a row with R2C1 and a box with R3C5. Remove the 3 from both.

How to find the Multi-Coloring

  1. Color clusters of one digit as in simple coloring. Give each separate cluster its own two colors.
  2. Find a place where a color of one cluster sees a color of the other.
  3. Remove the digit from cells that see the opposite colors of both.

Common questions

How is it different from simple coloring?

Simple coloring looks at the two colors inside one cluster. Multi-coloring looks at the colors of two separate clusters together.

What about three or more clusters?

Pair them up two at a time and use the same method.

Can an X-Chain explain it?

Yes. Add the weak link that joins the two clusters and they become one long X-Chain.
