SUDOKU TECHNIQUE · Basics

Hidden Pair

A Hidden Pair is two digits that can only go in the same two cells of a row, column or box. Every other candidate in those two cells can then be removed.

Where to look
One row, column or box
What to find
Two digits found in only two cells
What to remove
The other candidates in those cells

The Hidden Pair pattern

The diagram shows row 7 only. In row 7, 2 and 8 can only go in columns 3 and 7. Those two cells hold other candidates too, which is why the pair is called hidden.

2 and 8, found in only two cells Candidates to remove Candidates that stay

Why does this work?

Row 7 needs a 2, and it can only go in column 3 or column 7. The same holds for 8. So columns 3 and 7 have to share the 2 and the 8 between them.

With both cells taken by 2 and 8, no other digit fits there. So 1 and 4 come out of R7C3 and 5 and 9 out of R7C7. After that, the two cells form a naked pair of 2 and 8.

How to find the Hidden Pair

  1. Pick a row, column or box and look for digits that appear in only two cells.
  2. Check whether another digit appears in exactly the same two cells.
  3. If it does, remove every other candidate from those two cells.

Common questions

What if one of the digits appears in a third cell?

Then it is not a hidden pair. Both digits must appear in the same two cells and nowhere else.

Does it work in a box too?

Yes. The idea is the same in a row, a column or a box.

Why is it harder to see than a naked pair?

The other candidates in the two cells hide it. Ask "where can this digit go?" instead of "what can this cell be?" and it gets easier.
