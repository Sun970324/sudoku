SUDOKU TECHNIQUE · Wings

XYZ-Wing

An XYZ-Wing is a middle cell with three candidates and two cells it sees with two candidates each, all three sharing one digit. That digit can be removed from any cell that sees all three.

Cells needed
One with three candidates and two with two
Shared digit
One, in all three cells
What to remove
Cells that see all three

The XYZ-Wing pattern

The middle cell R2C2 holds 1, 4 and 7. R1C3 in the same box holds 1 and 7, and R2C7 in the same row holds 4 and 7. All three cells hold 7.

The middle's other candidates Digits shared with the middle 7, shared by all three 7 to remove

Why does this work?

The middle cell is 1, 4 or 7.

If the middle is 1 or 4If it is 1, R1C3 in the same box is 7. If it is 4, R2C7 in the same row is 7.
If the middle is 7The middle cell itself is 7.

Either way, one of the three cells is a 7. R2C3 sees all three (the same box as R1C3 and R2C2, the same row as R2C7), so it cannot be 7. Remove the 7.

How to find the XYZ-Wing

  1. Pick a cell with three candidates as the middle, say X, Y and Z.
  2. Among the cells it sees, find one with X and Z and one with Y and Z.
  3. Remove Z from every cell that sees all three.

Because the cell has to see all three, the cells to clear usually sit in the box the middle shares with one outer cell.

Common questions

How is it different from an XY-Wing?

An XY-Wing's middle has two candidates, so seeing the two outer cells is enough. An XYZ-Wing's middle can itself be Z, so the cell has to see the middle too.

Can both outer cells be in the same box?

Yes. They only need to see the middle, through a row, a column or a box.

What if the middle has four candidates?

That is the WXYZ-Wing, which ties four cells to the shared digit.
